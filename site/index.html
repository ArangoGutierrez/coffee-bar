What you see in the menu bar

Two glyphs, and the cup tells you which one at a glance. It reports whether an assertion is held right now — not what your agents are doing, which is what the panel is for. These are the real glyphs, and they are monochrome on purpose: macOS tints template images to match your menu bar, so they stay legible on any wallpaper and in either appearance.

idle Nothing is holding your Mac awake. It is free to sleep.
serving The wake assertion is held. Your Mac stays awake.

What it actually does

  • Under Auto, holds PreventUserIdleSystemSleep, bound to live agent session state.
  • Holds no display assertion by default. The screen still sleeps while the work continues. A Display control in the panel opts in when you do want the screen kept on.
  • Under Auto, releases when every agent is blocked on input, not when a timer expires.
  • Shows how many sessions are working, and which ones are waiting on you.
  • Retires a session whose agent crashed, so a dead process cannot pin the machine awake for ever.
  • Three positions — Off, Auto, On. Off is an absolute veto and outranks everything.
  • A battery floor, which ships at 20%: on battery, at or below 20%, it does not hold. The panel lets you move it.
No root. No password prompt. No kernel flags. No App Store. No network egress — it listens on a unix socket in a directory only your account can read, and posts nothing anywhere.
